# Sun Wukong — Full Card Draft (75 cards)

Fourth deep pass, completing the set alongside [Artemis](Artemis-CardDraft.md), [Thor](Thor-FullCardDraft.md), and [Anubis](Anubis-FullCardDraft.md). Supersedes the earlier [SunWukong-CardSketch.md](SunWukong-CardSketch.md) (kept for history) — every card from the sketch is carried forward here, expanded to full depth.

Recap: **Form** is a 3-state stance (Mortal/Beast/Immortal), no magnitude, purely categorical. Formalizing the passive modifiers referenced only qualitatively in the sketch, since card text below needs concrete numbers to reference:

- **Beast**: Attacks that normally deal damage once instead deal it twice. Attacks that already hit multiple times on their own are unaffected by this (a 5-hit Rare stays a 5-hit Rare in Beast Form — see Findings for why). Skills cost 1 additional Energy.
- **Immortal**: Strength and Block gained from cards are increased by 1. Attacks deal 3 less damage (minimum 1).
- **Mortal**: no modifier — neutral baseline/transition state.

Three archetypes from the sketch, kept as the organizing spine: **Beast Rush** (commit to Beast, double-hit tempo), **Immortal Ascension** (commit to Immortal, permanent-buff utility), **72 Changes** (cycle Form every turn, the switching itself is the payoff). A **Flex** tag marks cards any Wukong build reasonably wants, including the **Clone** sub-tool (replay the last card played) carried over from the sketch.

## Sanity Checks

**Count**: 3 Basic + 28 Common + 30 Uncommon + 14 Rare = 75. ✓

**Archetype distribution**: Beast Rush 18 (7C/7U/4R), Immortal Ascension 18 (7C/8U/3R), 72 Changes 19 (7C/8U/4R), Flex 17 (7C/7U/3R + 3 Basic). Even spread.

**Attack/Skill/Power split**: 25 Attack / 34 Skill / 16 Power ≈ 33% / 45% / 21%. Attack-light compared to the other three Blessings (Thor 41%, Anubis 39%) — Form-changing is naturally a Skill-shaped action (a stance change isn't a hit), so some skew is expected given Transformation/Utility is the stated focus, but 33% is a bigger deviation than Anubis's Skill-lean and worth a real look during balancing, not just a flavor justification. Flagging as a genuine finding, not explaining it away.

**Findings from going to full depth**:
- **Real problem found, not just a risk flagged — now resolved.** Beast Form's flat "Attacks hit twice" passive combined with multi-hit Rares (Ten Thousand Blows: 5 hits, Havoc in Heaven: 3 hits) would otherwise compound multiplicatively — Ten Thousand Blows in Beast Form would've resolved as 10 total hits from one card, not 5. The light sketch never caught this because it never had a multi-hit Rare to collide with the Form passive; only going to full depth surfaced it. **Ruling: Beast Form only adds a hit to Attacks that normally hit once. Cards that already hit multiple times on their own are unaffected.** Keeps every multi-hit card's power level legible on its own text, independent of which Form the player happens to be in — a 5-hit Rare always reads as a 5-hit Rare, full stop, rather than needing a mental "is Beast Form up?" check every time. Applied above in the Form recap and reflected in this file's numbers already (no per-card changes needed since none of the multi-hit cards' printed damage assumed the doubling).
- **72 Changes path cross-pollinates with the other two paths' entry-trigger Powers** (Berserker Form triggers on entering Beast, Jade Emperor's Favor triggers on entering Immortal) — a 72-Changes-leaning deck that cycles through all 3 Forms repeatedly can trigger both entry effects in the same turn. Read this as a positive, intentional soft-blend between paths (same spirit as Artemis's Predator's Focus or Thor's cross-archetype cards), not a problem — it rewards deck investment across paths rather than being a free combo.
- Immortal Ascension's own Common/Uncommon pool deliberately contains almost no Attacks (only Jade Blessing) — consistent with the Form's own passive weakening Attacks, so the path doesn't fight its own resource rule. Worth calling out as a design check that passed, not just an accident.
- Elixir of Immortality (Rare, Immortal Ascension) is a third instance of the "safety net" Rare pattern, same category as Thor's Deathless Rage and Anubis's Osiris's Rebirth. Three for three across the Blessings that have gone to full depth — strong signal this is a recurring pattern worth a single, consistent ruling (card vs. relic) rather than deciding it three separate times.

**Not attempted**: numeric tuning, same caveat as the other three Blessings.
